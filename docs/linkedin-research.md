# LinkedIn and publication research

Research date: 23 October 2026.

## Scope and limits

The requested LinkedIn profile at [uk.linkedin.com/in/chrys-dimitriou-eye-surgeon](https://uk.linkedin.com/in/chrys-dimitriou-eye-surgeon) returned a robots.txt denial. Publicly indexed individual posts, public repost excerpts and independent professional sources were used instead. No authentication, anti-bot or access restrictions were bypassed.

The inventory accounts for 63 relevant discoveries: 30 authored posts or authored-post excerpts, 25 third-party posts mentioning Chrys, and 8 comments on others' posts. These are not 63 articles and not a complete LinkedIn history. Some authored items are visible only inside another profile's public activity excerpt. Two UNITY CS posts may be versions of the same item, but were kept separate until an export can resolve them. A repeatedly indexed Katherin Awad ESCRS post was deduplicated.

Indexed LinkedIn pages displayed counts between 262 and 278 posts at different cache dates. These counts are not reliable current totals, but confirm that the discovered material is only a subset. Public search did not expose a verifiable standalone LinkedIn Pulse article by this surgeon. Similar names in unrelated specialties and industries were excluded from identity matching.

No exact original LinkedIn publication dates were verified. Relative ages differed between cached search and opened pages, so all originalDate fields are null. Dates mentioned inside a post are event dates, not proof of when it was published. Activity identifiers were not decoded to manufacture publication dates.

To complete the account history, the owner would need to provide an account export or a curated export of posts and articles containing original URLs, timestamps, full text, attachments, visibility and reuse permissions. Original conference abstracts and interview transcripts would resolve several partial leads.

## Medical and editorial review points

1. The six-month MINIject publisher page reports different IOP changes and medication-free percentages in the abstract, key summary and conclusion. Avoid those numerical claims until the author reconciles them. The article also reports adverse events and a limited sample, so claims of universal safety are inappropriate.
2. Several LinkedIn items use strong claims about preventing blindness, guaranteed drop freedom, best equipment, regional firsts or largest series. Their presence in a post is not independent verification. Their topics are now represented by source notes, with unsupported outcome and superiority claims omitted from the website summaries.
3. The INTERCIL item is a short clinical anecdote. It should not become a general treatment recommendation, and patient consent and rights must be reviewed before reusing details or imagery.
4. Manufacturer announcements are evidence of what the manufacturer said or scheduled, not neutral clinical evidence. An advisory affiliation is disclosed on the [iSTAR scientific adviser page](https://www.istar-medical.com/istar-medical/scientific-advisors/chrys-dimitriou/).
5. Published case reports, trials, reviews, conference abstracts and personal commentary must retain their distinct evidence types. A conference poster is not automatically a peer-reviewed full study.
6. Medical editorial transformations should be clinically reviewed before publication. Neutral bibliographic descriptions can identify verified work without inventing fresh advice. No article should promise an individual outcome.
7. Do not copy patient testimonials or case images from third-party posts. The BMJ case report explicitly restricts commercial reuse.
8. Older posts naming CyPass concern historical work. They must not imply it remains an available treatment; the peer-reviewed MINIject paper notes its market withdrawal.
